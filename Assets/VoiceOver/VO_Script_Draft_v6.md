## Rules

- The voice tells the story. It never gives instructions. "Look right" and "press A" belong to the hint line.
- One line per beat at most. Control beats (A3, B1 to B4, C2, D10, E3) get no story VO. A1 and A2 keep their story lines and also get a nudge. **[v6]** The dark matter moment in the journey (J3, first cosmic web) is the one exception: it gets one line before the press and one after it. It is the only moment where the press itself is the point of the story. (Until v5 this was E2 in the tutorial.)
- Paced at about 140 words per minute (2.3 words per second). Every line lists its estimated read time.
- Voice direction: calm, close, second person. Wonder, not lecture. **[v5]** **Female voice** (scientists' request). The whole set is re-recorded once in the new voice, after the scientists' yes/no pass.
- **[v3]** Say "absorb" and "wavelength" when describing what hydrogen does to light, not "take". Absorption removes the light at one wavelength, and that is why a color goes missing.
- Gated beats (A1, A2, C4, C5) hold until their line has finished. Cinematic beats use the durations in the table at the end.

### Control beats · optional nudge lines

> **Optional.** For discussion with the team. Each line names the exact control (Xbox pad): right stick = look, left stick = zoom, A = confirm / recentre, B = pause. They only play when the player needs one.

**Why.** GDD §5 keeps VO off control beats because hearing an instruction is slower than doing it. That holds for players who already get it. But the biggest failure in the May playtest was non-gamers not working out the camera, and text arrived faster than they could read it. A hint line alone may not reach exactly the players who need it.

**How they play.**

- Trigger: the beat has been open for 5 s and its gate is not met. Tunable per beat.
- Plays once per beat. Never repeats, never loops.
- If the player completes the gate mid-line, fade out over 0.3 s.
- The hint text stays on screen as now. The nudge adds sound; it does not replace the hint.
- This is a nudge timer, not a timeout. The beat still never advances on its own (§5, §12.3).

## Science to check

- **[v6] "The largest structure in the universe" (J3).** The cosmic web is the largest known structure. Its filaments are typically tens of megaparsecs long, a hundred million light-years and more, so "hundreds of millions of light-years" holds for its threads. Correct.
- **[v6] "Far too little gas here to pull itself into threads like these" (J3_DMoff_Before).** Ordinary matter is about one sixth of all matter, and until recombination it was held smooth by radiation. On its own it could not have collapsed into the web in the time available; dark matter's gravity had a head start, and the gas fell into it. A fair simplification.
- **[v6] "Drawn so you can see it" (both versions).** Dark matter gives off no light. The glow on screen is a drawing, and the lines say so, so no visitor leaves thinking dark matter can be seen.
- **[v6] "Outweighs ordinary matter five to one."** Same figure as J3 in v5 (≈ 5.3 : 1, Planck 2018). Correct.

- **[v5] "A cloud of hydrogen gas" (D4–D8).** The measurements reflect enormous numbers of atoms in a region of gas, not one atom; "cloud" is the scientists' preferred word.
- **[v5] "Outshines all the galaxy's stars" (A2).** A luminous quasar is typically 10 to 100 or more times brighter than its whole host galaxy. Correct.
- **[v5] "Five times heavier than ordinary matter" (J3).** Dark matter to ordinary matter ≈ 5.3 : 1 (Planck 2018). Correct.
- **[v5] "No stars light them" (J3b).** The gas in intergalactic filaments is mostly far from any galaxy, so it is not lit by stars; it is seen only by what it absorbs. A fair simplification.
- **[v5] "Maps of galaxies show only where stars shine" (J6_3).** A simplification of the scientists' point: galaxy surveys trace light from stars in the densest regions, while the forest traces diffuse gas through more of the volume.
- **[v5] "Like tree rings, each one marks a moment of your journey" (J9).** Each line comes from gas at a different distance along the path, so the lines are a record ordered in time. Kept away from the word "forest" on purpose.

- **"Less than two billion years after the Big Bang."** z = 3.62 → 1.74 Gyr after the Big Bang (Planck 2018). Correct.
- **"Nearly five times."** 1 + z = 4.62. Correct.
- **"Five to one."** Dark matter to ordinary matter ≈ 5.3 : 1 (Planck 2018). Correct.
- **"Eight minutes."** Sun–Earth light travel time is 8 min 20 s. Correct. **[v3]**
- **D5's "one wavelength" is a simplification.** It is one narrow band, Lyman-alpha at 121.6 nm, which is ultraviolet; the build starts the line inside UV (§12.2 item 0b, still open). **[v3]**
- **E2 Version A, "the gas drifts apart".** It is fair as a picture of what dark matter's gravity does, but no real cosmic web loses its dark matter. The line says "without dark matter", not "when dark matter disappears", so it stays a what-if. **[v3]**
- **J3b, "these yellow threads are hydrogen gas".** By mass, a cosmic web filament is mostly dark matter. Its ordinary matter is gas, about three quarters hydrogen by mass, plus the galaxies strung along it. Galaxy clusters sit at the nodes where filaments meet, not along the threads, so "clusters" would be wrong here. Almost all of that hydrogen is ionized; the tiny neutral fraction (around one atom in 10⁴ to 10⁵ at z ≈ 2 to 3) is still enough to make the Lyman-alpha forest. The line holds if the art keeps yellow for the gas on the ridges (CosmicWeb shader: purple network, yellow veins on the ridges, glow at the nodes). If the yellow points are meant as galaxies, say "threads" for the gas and leave the points out. **[v3]**
- **J3b, "each filament absorbs a wavelength".** It is the same Lyman-alpha line each time, landing at a different place on the spectrum because of redshift. That matches D6 and D8 in the tutorial. **[v3]**
- **J6's forest wording.** After z ≈ 2.46 the drawn Lyα lines are not in the light that reaches Earth (LAF & Redshift, open questions). J6 says "cut lines into your light", not "in the light that reaches Earth". Keep it that way.
- **"Half its present age" at seven billion years ago (J5).** 13.8 − 7 = 6.8 Gyr. Close enough for the read. Moved from J4 to J5 in v4, because the galaxy and the second cosmic web were both at seven billion years ago (100,000 years apart on the journey tracker), so the same date would have been said twice.
- **J0, "making stars faster every year".** At z ≈ 3.6 the universe's star-formation rate is still rising; it peaks around z ≈ 1.9 (Madau & Dickinson 2014). Correct. **[v4]**
- **J4, "faster than at any time before or since", ten billion years ago.** The cosmic star-formation peak, "cosmic noon", is at z ≈ 1.9, about 10.4 Gyr ago. Requires the journey tracker's Galaxy phase at 10 billion (it was 7). **[v4]**
- **J4b, "a hundred thousand years to cross a galaxy like this one".** Light crosses a Milky Way–sized galaxy (about 100,000 light-years) in about 100,000 years. "Like this one" covers the art not stating a size. "Less than the blink of an eye" is a comparison: 100,000 years is 1/120,000 of the journey, so if the journey took an hour, the galaxy would take 0.03 s, and a blink is 0.1 to 0.4 s. The line never says light slows down; it does not. **[v4]**
